# Polynomial algebra and finite algebraic extensions

Developed on 2026-09-22 in Cubist's cubical C kernel. The finite linear-algebra,
polynomial, algebraic-extension, and separability/normality results below are
proved in source; none is supplied as a new axiom. This increment does not
claim the full fundamental theorem of Galois theory.

## Representation and polynomial proofs

A polynomial is a coefficient function `Nat -> af_carrier(K)` with mere finite
support. Equality is coefficientwise, not equality of the induced functions
on K. This distinction is essential over finite fields. The cutoff is the
least support bound: zero for the zero polynomial, degree plus one otherwise.
Division explicitly excludes the zero divisor. Root bounds explicitly require
a nonzero polynomial.

The development checks convolution, the commutative ring laws, leading
coefficients of products, degree addition, and the integral-domain property.
Least degree and division prove that nonzero ideals are principal. Bézout and
irreducible factors follow. Irreducible means nonzero, nonunit, and having a
unit factor in every factorization; merely asserting the absence of base-field
roots is not used as a general irreducibility criterion.

`PolynomialResidueField(K,M,irreducible)` uses canonical remainders below the
modulus degree as its carrier. It stays in U0, with no quotient postulate or
resizing step. Bézout supplies inverses; uniqueness permits extraction from
mere existence without Choice. Coefficient arrays give the actual finite
basis 1, X, …, X^(d−1), proving the extension degree.

The monic minimal polynomial is the unique normalized generator of the
annihilator ideal. It divides every annihilating polynomial. Its definition
recovers data from unique existence; it does not choose an arbitrary generator.

## Embeddings, splitting, and separability

A root determines a unique embedding of an irreducible residue field. The
embedding type includes the commuting triangle over the base. It is equivalent,
and by univalence equal, to the root type. A simple field generated by an
algebraic element is identified with its residue-field presentation as a
complete field, including the operations.

A vector basis supplies finitely many field generators. Adjoin the first
generator by its minimal polynomial, enlarge the base, and repeat on the tail.
This proves both finite generation/algebraicity ⇒ finiteness and extension of
embeddings. A primitive-element theorem is not assumed.

`AlgebraicallyClosed(T)` is an explicit hypothesis that every nonzero nonunit
polynomial over T merely has a root. It is **not an added axiom** and these
modules do **not construct an algebraic closure**. The embedding-extension and
general embedding-count theorems quantify over any T with this property.
Extension existence is propositional truncation, not a chosen embedding.

Splitting-field existence itself needs no algebraically closed target: induction
on degree adjoins roots in finite extensions, then restricts to the field
generated by the roots. Generation is part of the final witness, distinguishing
a splitting field from a larger field where the polynomial happens to split.

`NormalExtension` consists of algebraicity and the standard embedding condition:
for every common target and two embeddings of L agreeing on K, their images
agree. The formal law states image inclusion for universally quantified ordered
pairs, so both inclusions follow. The equivalence with the irreducible-polynomial
splitting criterion has not yet been formalized.

A separable polynomial is nonzero and admits a factorization into deg p distinct
linear factors in some extension. Separable elements are defined using their
minimal polynomials; a separable extension has every element separable. The
finite root-generated subfield embeds into any algebraically closed target,
transporting that distinct factorization. Removing a chosen linear factor,
canceling it from a divisibility equation, and induction prove the needed
separability result for divisors. No derivative criterion is assumed.

`HasCardinality(A,n)` means mere existence of a bijection Fin(n) → A. Embeddings
of a tower form a dependent sum over embeddings of the lower field. The library
proves equality of this type with the total embedding type by univalence. It
proves finite choice by induction, counts uniformly sized fibers, and transports
the count to the total embedding type. The tower law then yields exactly [L:K].
This proof handles arbitrary finite separable extensions, not only simple ones.

## Assumptions and universes

Field and vector carriers remain in U0; bundled fields and predicate subfields
live in U1. The new algebra uses existing LEM(U0) and propositional truncation.
There is no new mathematical axiom, kernel rule, or language syntax.

Finite spanning extraction, division, residue fields, minimal polynomials, and
finite counting do not require Choice. Some existence interfaces quantify over
bundled fields and use the existing `Truncate(U1)`/`FieldExists` interface; its
universe-lowering elimination strength is inherited and must not be described
as a new predicative construction. Tests audit the actual axiom dependencies.
Univalence and function extensionality are computationally derived by the
current cubical kernel, not extra univalence axioms.

## Validation

The focused suites are `tests/finite-spanning.test.mjs`,
`tests/polynomial-algebra.test.mjs`, and `tests/algebraic-extensions.test.mjs`.
They check the general statements and allowed assumptions, exercise the
concrete F4 polynomial, and reject division/root bounds with missing nonzero
hypotheses, embeddings with no closed-target hypothesis, an incorrect embedding
count, and a false root.

Run `npm test`, `node tools/format-mathscript.mjs --check`, and
`node tools/audit-cubical.mjs --all`. The bounded diagnostic audit adds
`--diagnostic-budget=100000000`; exhausting that bound is a conversion-performance
failure, not a mathematical counterexample. Final run results are recorded in
the resumption checkpoint.

## Next toward full Galois

The full roadmap retains additional work: derivative/repeated-root criteria,
normality's equivalent polynomial and finite Galois characterizations,
embedding counts in splitting targets without a supplied algebraic closure,
Artin's fixed-field degree theorem, and the general finite Galois correspondence
with its normal-subgroup quotient statement. Distinguished-class instances
still need to be connected to these new algebraic and separability interfaces.
Arbitrary infinite algebraic extension of embeddings and algebraic-closure
existence are explicitly outside the initial finite Galois scope; they require
a separate future request.
